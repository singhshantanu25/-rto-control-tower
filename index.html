    1,000 orders pass the sort-centre check. Move the hold line to see the trade-off: a held parcel that would have been delivered is a lost sale.

    Where to draw the hold line

    A buyer who doesn't reply is more likely to refuse. We only hold their parcel when shipping it is expected to cost more than holding it:

    hold if P(RTO | no reply) ≥ (V − ₹39 + ₹6) ÷ (V + ₹120)

    ₹39 is the network still ahead of the parcel, ₹6 sends it back from the sort centre, ₹120 is the reverse trip. V is what a delivered order is worth.

    Hold line60%

    Value per 1,000 orders = logistics cost saved + deliveries gained × ₹265 AOV. It peaks near 60%. Using Meesho's revenue per order (₹74) gives a 21% line, which saves more money but cancels far more real orders.

    Solution 2: Valmo Resell Nearby

    What happens to a refused parcel

    A sealed refused parcel waits up to 72 hours at the last-mile hub and is offered to nearby buyers of the same item, from the same seller at the same price. The seller makes a sale and Meesho avoids the ₹120 return trip; unsold parcels go back in bulk.

    Match rate vs hub size

    Selected demand caseThe other case

    How we got the base case: a typical hub delivers ~500 parcels a day, so it sees ~1,500 orders in 72 hours. A refused size-M kurti only re-homes if one of them is the same seller, item and size. If the top 1% of Meesho's ~2 million seller-item-size variants take ~30% of orders, bestsellers match often and the long tail almost never: about 7% at a 500-a-day hub and ~9% network-wide, since bigger hubs carry more parcels.

    • Only sealed parcels with intact tamper tape. No innerwear, cosmetics or opened boxes.
    • Same seller, same item, same size, at the seller's own price. Meesho never discounts it.
    • The buyer who refused (and their address and device) can't buy it back.
    • The seller owns the parcel the whole time. Valmo only stores and delivers it.
    • The hub partner is paid ₹5 per re-homed parcel and ₹2 for each one stored that doesn't sell.

    How the live numbers are computed

    At the Base, Low and High settings, every figure is taken straight from our Python run of 1 million orders, the same numbers as in our deck. If you change a slider, the browser simulates 40,000 orders twice (your settings and the defaults, using the same random draws) and adds the difference to the Python figure. Those custom results are approximate.
